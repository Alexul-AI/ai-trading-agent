# Go-Live Criteria

Checklist for deciding when this bot is ready to trade with real capital (`TRADE_MODE=live`). Written 2026-07-09.

## Why this exists

"It's been running a while and looks fine" is not a decision process. A ~$13,444 realized loss from an automated trading incident on 2026-06-22 to 2026-06-29 happened under exactly that kind of informal judgment, before this checklist existed. This document replaces gut-feel with measurable, checkable gates.

## Gates (all must pass before going live)

### 1. Continuous dry-run operation
- [ ] Autopilot running in scheduled mode (not manual triggers) for **4-6 consecutive weeks**
- [ ] Covers more than one kind of market condition, not just a single trending or single flat stretch

### 2. Sample size

**This gate was written for a daily-signal strategy and does not fit ETF Rotation's actual monthly-rebalance cadence** - updated 2026-08-08, not rewritten from scratch, since the original reasoning (below this, results are statistically indistinguishable from noise) still holds, only the unit of measurement changes.

- [ ] Original criterion, still applicable if a daily-cadence strategy (e.g. a future Trend Participation candidate, `docs/product/ROADMAP.md` Phase 5) is ever live: at least **20-30 closed paper trades** with `AUTOPILOT_EXECUTE_TRADES=true` (still paper mode)
- [ ] **ETF Rotation-specific criterion (current live strategy)**: at least **3 full monthly rebalance cycles** before treating results as informative at all, at least **6 full monthly rebalance cycles** as the more reliable minimum before any promotion/config decision (e.g. `candidate-hold3`) or go-live discussion. Mirrors the read criteria already established for `backtest-etf-rotation-forward-validation.ts`'s CSV log (`docs/product/ROADMAP.md` Phase 2) - the same threshold, applied to the real account instead of the parallel forward-validation simulation.
- At real monthly cadence with 5-8 legs per full rebalance (SELL+BUY per changed pick), the daily-strategy trade count (20-30) would take 6-12+ months to reach - not a useful gate for this strategy as currently deployed. Below the 3-cycle floor, results are statistically indistinguishable from noise (confirmed in the original backtest-sweep experiments this gate was written from: 5-8 trade samples showed "improvements" that vanished once the sample grew - the same logic, just counted in rebalance cycles instead of individual trades for a monthly strategy).
- **Read this gate together with the caveat at the end of this document** - a rebalance cycle where a leg was left off-target (like 2026-08-03's QQQ rejection) still counts toward the cycle count, but the resulting portfolio state does not represent the strategy's intended, fully-executed target - see "Operational readiness vs. strategy fidelity" below.

### 3. Risk mechanisms verified live, not just in backtest
- [ ] Stop-loss has fired at least once in paper mode and behaved correctly
- [ ] Take-profit has fired at least once in paper mode and behaved correctly
- [ ] Position/cash caps have been observed blocking an over-sized buy at least once
- [ ] Bucket concentration cap (built 2026-07-11) has been observed blocking a same-bucket over-concentration at least once
- [ ] Portfolio circuit breaker (built 2026-07-11) has been observed tripping at least once and correctly blocking new BUYs while still allowing SELLs
- [ ] If `AUTOPILOT_ALLOW_FRACTIONAL_SHARES` is enabled (built 2026-07-11, off by default): at least one fractional/notional BUY has gone through paper trading, and its cycle-based STOP_LOSS/TAKE_PROFIT exit (no broker-side bracket for these positions) has been observed firing correctly - this is a genuinely different exit mechanism than the whole-share bracket path and needs its own live verification, not just unit-test coverage

These are trading-behavior gates. A separate, platform/ops-level checklist - deploy topology, restart/persistence behavior, order-idempotency durability, emergency stop - lives in `docs/ops/PAPER_INFRASTRUCTURE_GATE.md` and must also clear before `AUTOPILOT_EXECUTE_TRADES=true`, not just before live capital. As of 2026-07-15: 7 of its 8 items are materially addressed (circuit-breaker and order-idempotency restart persistence code-fixed and regression-tested; single Render instance, persistent disk, and no-duplicate-worker-cycles confirmed via a real Render dashboard check - scaling is structurally disabled while this service's disk is attached). The 8th (alerts surviving a real restart while halted) is explicitly accepted as a **deferred risk scoped to paper execution only** - not resolved, and must be revisited (real observation or a deliberate fire drill) before any micro-live capital, per `docs/ops/PAPER_INFRASTRUCTURE_GATE.md` item 6.

### 4. Experimental filters excluded from the readiness decision
- [ ] `AUTOPILOT_SENTIMENT_FILTER` and `AUTOPILOT_INSIDER_FILTER` are either left off, or their block events are tracked and evaluated separately from the core strategy's results
- These signals cannot be backtested (the underlying APIs only return current data, not point-in-time history), so their effect on readiness must never be assumed positive by default.

### 5. Account-level circuit breaker
- [x] A maximum portfolio drawdown threshold is defined and enforced (-15% equity from peak, `AUTOPILOT_MAX_DRAWDOWN_FROM_PEAK_PERCENT`, halts new BUYs automatically - built 2026-07-11)
- Blocks new BUYs only, never SELLs, same as the existing per-trade stop-loss philosophy. Peak equity is derived each cycle from Alpaca's own account history rather than tracked in a local file we could lose. See `CLAUDE.md`'s "Architecture facts" for the full design. Still needs to actually *fire* at least once in paper mode before this counts as verified, not just built - see gate 3.
- **2026-07-13**: the sticky/no-auto-recovery design got its first real stress test in `backtest-portfolio.ts`'s next-open model, where it tripped once (2025-04-08) and then locked out new BUYs for 78% of a 406-day window, turning +20.66% into -9.68%. A backtest-only follow-up simulated several reset policies against that same trip - every one tested (reset after 1/3/5/10 trading days, or once drawdown recovered above -10%) turned the result positive (+5% to +8%) with no worse max drawdown than the current sticky policy on that one window.
- **2026-07-13, later same day**: ran the same comparison across 5 windows (`backtest-portfolio-multiwindow.ts`, new), not just the one above - **the single-window finding reverses in a real bear market**. The breaker only tripped under D0 in 2 of 5 windows tested. In the current window, every reset policy still looked good (as above). In a 2022-bear-heavy window, the fixed-timer reset policies (1/3/5/10 trading days) had a *slightly better return* than sticky/no-reset but a **meaningfully worse max drawdown** (-22% to -24% vs. sticky's -16%) - resetting early let the strategy re-enter positions that then kept falling with the broader market. The recovery-gated policy (reset only once drawdown improves above -10%) never triggered in that bear window and matched the sticky default exactly - arguably correct behavior, not a failure. See `CLAUDE.md`'s "Strategy performance reality" for full numbers. This is a genuine argument *against* a simple fixed-timer auto-reset as a default policy, and a mild argument *for* a recovery-gated one over a timer-based one - but still only 2 windows where the breaker actually fired, nowhere near enough to inform a live decision on its own.
- **2026-07-14**: since no reset-timing policy proved reliably safe above, built visibility instead of any auto-reset: a once-per-day Telegram reminder while halted, an audit log (`data/circuit-breaker-audit.jsonl`, trip/reminder/reset events), a required `reason` field + Telegram confirmation on the existing manual reset endpoint, and a new `GET /api/autopilot/circuit-breaker/review` endpoint (halt status, drawdown, days halted, recently-blocked signals, positions/cash) for an eventual dashboard panel. The trip alert and the reset endpoint itself already existed - this only added the reminder, the audit trail, and the review data. No auto-reset of any kind was added. See `CLAUDE.md`'s "Architecture facts" for the full design. Frontend banner/panel UI is a separate follow-up, not built yet.

### 6. Capital increases are earned, not scheduled
- [ ] Each tranche ($100 -> $1,000 -> $10,000) is unlocked by the previous tranche's own track record clearing these same gates again - not by a calendar date
- **Real, unresolved blocker for the $100-$250 first live tranche (`docs/product/ROADMAP.md` Phase 4), added 2026-08-08**: ETF Rotation's live sizing is whole-shares-only (`computeRebalanceOrders`, no fractional/notional support, unlike the baseline strategy's opt-in `AUTOPILOT_ALLOW_FRACTIONAL_SHARES` fallback). At SPY/QQQ prices around $750-770 and `holdCount=2`'s 50%-per-slot weighting, $250 of capital (or even $1,000) cannot buy even 1 whole share of either pick. As currently built, ETF Rotation is not deployable at all at the Phase 4 tranche-1 capital level - this needs to be resolved (fractional/notional support for the rotation path, a lower-priced universe, or an explicit decision to start the live tranche higher than $250) before Phase 4 can proceed with this strategy, independent of whether the other gates above have cleared.
- **Update (2026-08-08, PR #80) - the blocker is now measured, not just described, and it splits into two independent decisions.** Built (off by default, unreachable from the live worker) fractional/notional BUY support for `computeRebalanceOrders`/`etfRotationExecution.ts`, plus a real 160-combination simulation (5 windows x 2 config variants x $100/$250/$500/$1000 x ramp on/off x fractional on/off) against live Alpaca data. Result: with **no ramp cap**, fractional support fixes the $100-$250 tranche completely (stranded-slot rate drops from 100% to 0%, average exposure rises to ~90-97%). With the **current live 2% ramp cap left on**, fractional support does **not** fix it - the ramp's dollar ceiling shrinks a fallback position to a few dollars (clears the binary "not stranded" check but leaves average exposure at only 3-4%), and for tickers where whole-share sizing already succeeds, the ramp's share-based ceiling still floors to 0 regardless. **This means small-tranche viability is gated on two separate, independent decisions, not one: (1) whether to enable fractional/notional for ETF Rotation, and (2) whether/how much to raise `AUTOPILOT_ETF_ROTATION_RAMP_MAX_POSITION_PERCENT`.** Enabling fractional alone, without also revisiting the ramp, would not actually unlock the tranche. Both decisions stay frozen, same as every other risk-surface change, until at least the September rebalance-cycle read (gate 2's 3+/6+ criterion). Full grid: `backend/data/backtest-reports/etf-rotation/etf-rotation-small-tranche-simulation-report.md` (gitignored, local).

## Operational readiness vs. strategy fidelity (added 2026-08-08)

Two different questions get asked about the same paper account, and they must not be answered with the same number:

- **"Is the system operating correctly?"** - gates, audit trail, alerts, idempotency, no duplicate orders, no unreviewed halt. This is what gates 1-6 above are actually about.
- **"Is the strategy holding the portfolio it's supposed to hold?"** - whether the account's actual positions match `decideRotationTargets`' computed weights. This is a separate question, and currently the honest answer is **no, not closely**, for three independent reasons that all need to be read into any results before a promotion or go-live decision:

1. **Ramp cap vs. full-weight backtests.** Live paper execution runs under `AUTOPILOT_ETF_ROTATION_RAMP_MAX_POSITION_PERCENT=2` (2% of equity per position) - a deliberate ramp-up safety measure, not a bug. But `backtest-etf-rotation-forward-validation.ts`'s CSV log (the ongoing forward-validation referenced in gate 2) simulates the **full** target weights (50%/holdCount, no ramp cap). These are not the same portfolio: on an ~$88k paper account, 2% sizing means each position is roughly $1,500-1,600 (~1.7% of equity), nowhere near the strategy's intended 50%. Live paper right now is validating **execution mechanics** (order submission, gates, audit, the repair-flow) at token size, not the strategy's actual return/drawdown characteristics at its intended weights. There is currently no documented schedule or criteria for raising the ramp cap over time - that is a separate, not-yet-answered question, distinct from the capital-tranche gates above.
2. **August is a partial/off-target month.** The 2026-08-03 rebalance left QQQ unbought (see the Status section above) - as of this writing, still unresolved. Any read of August paper results needs to explicitly account for this: the account did not hold its intended target portfolio for the month, so August's realized numbers reflect an accidentally-underweight, partially-executed state, not the strategy's designed behavior. Whether or not the repair endpoint (PR #71) is ever used to fix this, September's reading (see gate 2's 3/6-cycle criterion) must state plainly whether the months being counted were clean or partial - a "3 clean cycles" bar and a "3 cycles, one of them partial" bar are not the same evidence.
3. **Both live and forward-validation currently read `adjustment=raw` bars, and research says that's probably not neutral.** PRs #76/#77 (2026-08-08, `docs/product/ROADMAP.md` Phase 2) found that `adjustment=raw` vs `adjustment=all` (dividend/distribution-adjusted) changes ETF Rotation's monthly top-2 picks on 10.7-13.1% of rebalance dates, with a pre-declared-criteria verdict of "adjusted likely better" (mechanism confirmed, Calmar favors `all` in 16/20 window/variant/model combinations). **Nothing about live execution or forward-validation changed** - both still read `raw`, this is a research finding, not yet an implemented decision (see `ROADMAP.md` Phase 2 for the risk-tiered plan for if/how this gets addressed). But it means September's forward-validation read, whatever it shows, is itself being measured on a methodology this project's own research now leans against - a caveat to state plainly alongside that reading, not a reason to distrust it outright.

## Reference numbers from the 2026-07-09 backtesting session (extended 2026-07-11)

- Baseline strategy (`v1.2-confluence-scoring`), 10 tickers, 900-day window: roughly +1.4% to +2%/year on average, highly variable by ticker (-1.4% to +6%)
- No parameter tweak tested that day (wider position sizing, post-stop-loss cooldown, trend-level filter, trend-slope filter) produced a robust, validated improvement over baseline
- Buy-and-hold outperformed the strategy substantially on trending tickers (e.g. AMD +206%, TSLA +87% over 900 days vs. the strategy's single-digit returns) - this strategy trades upside for smaller drawdowns, it is not a growth strategy, and that tradeoff should inform expectations, not just the go-live decision
- **2026-07-11**: ticker universe expanded to 13 (sector/asset-class diversified) - performance held in the same ballpark on the same window. ATR-based stops and a portfolio regime filter were both built and backtested (two non-overlapping 900-day windows for the regime filter, one covering the 2022 bear market) - neither showed strong enough, consistent-enough evidence to become the default; both ship off. Full numbers in `CLAUDE.md`'s "Strategy performance reality" section - not duplicated here to avoid the two files drifting out of sync with each other.
- **2026-07-13**: all prior backtests (including the numbers above) simulated each ticker independently with its own $10,000 - none of them modeled the bucket cap, portfolio circuit breaker, sell-fraction throttle, or daily kill switch, since those only exist for a shared account. `backtest-portfolio.ts` (new) runs all 13 tickers as one real portfolio with every one of those layers applied. First 900-day run showed the bucket cap alone binding 184 times - see `CLAUDE.md`'s "Strategy performance reality" for the full numbers. One run, not yet a validated finding.
